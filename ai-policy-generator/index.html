Staff policy

AI use policy

1. Purpose

AI tools can save us time on writing, research, and routine work. This policy explains how our company uses them safely.

It applies to everyone who works with us, including part-time staff and contractors. If something here is unclear, ask the owner before you go ahead.

2. Approved tools

You may use these kinds of AI tools for work:

  • Chat assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, for drafting, summarizing, and brainstorming.
  • AI features built into our email and office tools, such as writing help and summaries.

Use a work account when we have one, not a personal one. Any tool not on this list needs approval first.

3. What never goes into AI

Never paste, upload, or type these into any AI tool, even an approved one:

  • Customer personal information, such as names, emails, phone numbers, or addresses.
  • Payment details, such as card or bank account numbers.
  • Passwords, access keys, or login codes.
  • Employee records, such as pay, reviews, health, or personal details.
  • Contracts and agreements, or anything we promised to keep private.
  • Financial records, such as statements, tax files, or account details.

AI built into our own approved email and office accounts may see what's already there. Don't copy these details into any other AI tool.

Not sure if something is sensitive? Leave it out, or remove the names and details first. When in doubt, ask.

4. Checking AI's work

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. You are responsible for anything you use, whether AI helped or not.

  • Read every AI draft before you use it.
  • Check facts, names, dates, and links against a source you trust.
  • Any number that matters, such as a price, total, or deadline, gets a second check by another person or a second source.
  • Use AI to read and draft. Don't let it change records, move money, or delete anything on its own.
  • If the AI seems unsure, or the answer looks wrong, stop and ask a person.

5. Customer-facing use

AI may draft messages to customers. A person reads and approves every one before it is sent.

Leave out the customer's name, email, and other personal details when you ask for a draft, and add them yourself before sending.

Nothing reaches a customer without a person's yes. AI never makes a promise, offers a discount, or agrees to anything on our behalf.

6. New tools and approvals

Want to try a new AI tool, or use one in a new way? Ask the owner first. Before saying yes, the owner checks:

  • What the tool does with our information, and whether it uses it for training.
  • That we can use a work account with a strong password and two-step login.
  • Who will use it, and for what.
  • What happens if it makes a mistake, and who will notice.

Approved tools get added to this policy.

7. Mistakes and reporting

Mistakes happen. Speaking up early keeps them small.

  • If sensitive information went into an AI tool by accident, tell the owner right away.
  • If something AI helped write went out with an error, tell the owner the same day so we can fix it.
  • Any AI tool that runs on its own must alert a person when it fails or stops working.
  • Reporting an honest mistake quickly will not get you in trouble.

8. Review date

We review this policy twice a year, and whenever we add a new AI tool. AI changes fast, so this policy will too.

Next review: 6 months from the date this policy is adopted.

Questions? Ask the owner.

Common questions

Does a small business need an AI policy?
Yes, if anyone on your team uses AI tools for work. One page tells staff which tools are fine, what must never go in, and who checks the results. It takes minutes and helps prevent the most common mistakes.
What should an AI use policy include?
The tools staff may use, the information that must never go into AI, how people check AI's work, whether AI may write to customers, who approves new tools, and when the policy gets reviewed.
