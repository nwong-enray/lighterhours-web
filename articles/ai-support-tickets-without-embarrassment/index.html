How we let AI answer support tickets without embarrassing anyone

Customers dread bad support bots, and they have reason to. How we built AI that answers support tickets without ever bluffing, and what changed.

By the Lighter Hours team·October 8, 2026·3 min read

You know the feeling from the other side. You're stuck, you finally reach out for help, and a chatbot answers something you didn't ask, with total confidence. You type "agent" four times. By the end you're angrier at the company than at the original problem.

Your customers know that feeling too. That's what makes AI support scary for a business owner.

The fear is justified

  • A Gartner survey found 64% of customers would prefer companies didn't use AI for customer service, and 53% would consider switching to a competitor over it. Their top worry: it makes it harder to reach a person.
  • When Air Canada's website chatbot gave a grieving passenger wrong refund information, a tribunal held the airline responsible for what its bot said.

And yet Gartner also predicts AI will resolve 80% of common service issues on its own by 2029. Both things are true. AI support is coming, and done carelessly, it costs you customers.

How we built it differently

When we built an AI that answers support tickets inside a real, busy company, we spent more time on when it should stay quiet than on what it should say.

  • It only answers from the company's own approved help content. No improvising, no guessing from the internet.
  • When it isn't sure, it doesn't bluff. The customer gets a quick note that their message was received, and a person takes it from there.
  • Some topics always go to a person. Anything involving money or a promise the AI isn't allowed to make never gets an AI answer.
  • It was tested on real tickets it had never seen before it was trusted, not on hand-picked examples.
  • It gets better every week from the team's corrections, and it never learns from its own answers, so mistakes can't compound.

What changed

Over 60 days, about 24% of tickets were fully solved by the AI with no staff time. That sounds modest, and it's honest. Each of those was a person who got a right answer in a minute instead of waiting in a queue. Every ticket the AI wasn't sure about still reached a human.

The team didn't lose its job. It lost the repetitive part of it, and got more time for the customers who really needed a person.

If you're thinking about this

  • Start with your most common questions. If you don't have clear written answers yet, writing them is the first project, not the AI.
  • Decide up front which topics a person always handles.
  • Measure one number first, like time to first reply, so you'll know whether it worked.

AI can take real weight off a support team. The trick is building it to know its limits, and checking it like a new hire instead of trusting it like a vending machine.
