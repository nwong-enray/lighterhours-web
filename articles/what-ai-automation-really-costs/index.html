What AI automation really costs a small business

You've heard AI is expensive. The AI part is usually cents per job. Here's where the real cost lives, and what the research says.

By the Lighter Hours team·October 8, 2026·3 min read

Maybe you've sat through a demo, nodded along, and then seen a quote that made your stomach drop. Or maybe you never asked, because you assumed AI is for companies with a data team and a seven-figure budget. Either way, the price feels like a wall.

Here's the honest picture, from what we've built and what the research says.

The AI itself got cheap, fast

The Stanford AI Index 2025 found that the cost of running an AI model as capable as early ChatGPT dropped more than 280-fold between late 2022 and late 2024. That matches what we see. Most jobs we run cost a few cents to a few dollars each in AI fees. Turning a screen recording into a step-by-step how-to guide costs about 3 cents.

So why aren't more small businesses using it? The US Census Bureau found that only about 1 in 5 US businesses use AI, and the smallest firms lag furthest behind. In an earlier Census study, the most common reason businesses gave for not using it was simple: they didn't think it applied to them.

It usually does. The gap isn't the price of the AI. It's knowing where it fits.

Where the real cost lives

Access. Most of a project's time goes into getting the keys to your own systems: the right permissions, your vendors' approval, accounts set up properly. It's unglamorous, and it's usually the hardest part.

Rules. Someone has to decide what the AI may send, write, and spend. "Nothing goes to a customer without a person's yes" is a rule. Writing rules down is cheap. Skipping them is how AI projects become horror stories.

Keeping it running. Automations break quietly. Something changes, a job stops, and nobody notices for days. We learned that the hard way. Anything your business depends on needs someone watching it and an alert when it fails.

Your team's time. Someone has to own each tool, check its work early on, and speak up when it's wrong. Plan for a few hours a week at the start.

Can't a $20 AI plan just do all this?

Today's AI can do a surprising amount of the building itself. What it can't do on its own is decide what's safe for your business, sign in as you, or notice when something quietly stops working next month. The tools are cheap. The judgment is the work.

A rough budget for one workflow

For one focused workflow, like answering common customer questions or a daily numbers report:

  • AI fees: usually a few dollars to a few tens of dollars a month.
  • Setup: a one-time project, mostly spent on access and testing.
  • Upkeep: a little time each month to keep it healthy.

If someone quotes you a big monthly software fee for something that could run on tools you already own, ask what you're paying for.
