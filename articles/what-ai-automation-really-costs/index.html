What AI automation really costs a small business

The AI part is usually cents per job. The real costs are access, rules, and someone to fix it when it breaks. An honest breakdown.

October 8, 2026

People expect AI automation to be expensive because the headlines are about billion-dollar models. For a small or mid-size business, the AI itself is usually the cheapest part.

The AI part costs cents

Most of the jobs I run cost somewhere between a few cents and a few dollars each in AI fees. A few real examples:

  • Turning a recording of someone clicking through a tool into a step-by-step how-to guide: about 3 cents.
  • Cross-checking a leadership analysis with three different AI models: under $0.50 total.
  • Polishing a paragraph of writing: done in under a second, using one of the cheapest models available.

Many of the surrounding tools are free, too. Google's Analytics, Search Console, Gmail, Drive and Sheets APIs cost nothing for normal business use. A typical AI chat plan runs about $20 a month.

The real costs

Access. Most of the time on a project goes into getting the keys to your own systems: admin rights, permission from your software vendors to read your data, and accounts set up properly. It's slow and unglamorous, and it's usually the hardest part.

Rules. Someone has to decide what the AI may send, what it may write, and what it may spend. "Nothing goes to a customer without a person's yes" is a rule. "Never change the database" is a rule. Writing these down is cheap. Skipping them is how AI projects become horror stories.

Keeping it running. This is the cost people forget. Automations break quietly: a password expires, a vendor changes a screen, a job stops running and nobody notices. I learned this firsthand. Some of my early jobs ran on a laptop and missed about one morning in five, and some cloud jobs had no failure alerts. Anything a business depends on should run in the cloud and shout when it fails.

Your team's time. Someone has to own each tool, check its work early on, and speak up when it's wrong. Plan for a few hours a week at the start.

Can't a $20 AI plan just do all this?

Today's AI agents can do a surprising amount of the setup themselves: writing the code, clicking through settings, wiring services together. What they can't do on their own is decide what's safe, sign in as you, pay for things, or notice when something stops working next month.

So the honest answer: the tools are cheap, and the wiring and judgment are the work.

A rough budget for one workflow

For one focused workflow, like answering common support questions or a daily numbers report:

  • AI fees: usually a few dollars to a few tens of dollars a month.
  • Setup: a one-time project, mostly spent on access and testing.
  • Upkeep: a little time each month, yours or someone's, to keep it healthy.

If someone quotes you a big monthly software fee for something that could run on tools you already own, ask what you're paying for.
