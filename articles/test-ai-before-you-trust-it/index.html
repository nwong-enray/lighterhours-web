Will AI mess up with my customers? Test it before you trust it

Chatbots have promised refunds, sold a car for $1, and told businesses to break the law. The three checks we run before any AI talks to a customer.

By the Lighter Hours team·October 23, 2026·2 min read

The question we hear most from owners isn't "can AI do this?" It's "what if it says something wrong to a customer?" That's exactly the right worry. One wrong answer can cost more than every hour the AI ever saved.

It has happened, publicly

  • Air Canada's chatbot told a grieving passenger he could claim a refund the airline's policy didn't allow. A tribunal held the airline responsible.
  • A car dealership's website chatbot was talked into agreeing to sell a new Chevy Tahoe for $1.
  • New York City's official business chatbot told owners to break the law, from taking workers' tips to refusing cash.

None of these companies set out to embarrass themselves. They trusted the AI before they tested it. The US government's standards agency, NIST, now names pre-deployment testing as a core safeguard for exactly this reason.

Our three checks, in plain terms

1. A quiet trial run. First, the AI works in the background where no customer sees it, while your team answers as usual. Then we compare: where did it agree with your people, and where did it get it wrong? It costs your customers nothing.

2. A safety test it has to pass perfectly. Next come the questions where a wrong answer hurts: refunds it can't promise, an upset customer, someone asking about an account that isn't theirs. "Mostly right" isn't good enough. Our most recent customer-facing helper passed 29 of 29 with zero safety failures. If it had failed one, it wouldn't have gone live.

3. A match against your best people. For answers that take judgment, we check whether the AI makes the same call your experienced staff already made. In one recent check, it matched the support rep's decision 47 times out of 47. Only then does it get near anything involving a customer's account.

After launch, keep checking

Passing the tests isn't the finish line. Early on, a person reads what goes out. And when the AI isn't sure, it hands the customer to a person instead of guessing.

What to ask anyone selling you AI

  • How will we see its answers before customers do?
  • Which mistakes would hurt us most, and how is it tested on those?
  • What does it do when it isn't sure?

If the answers are vague, the AI isn't ready for your customers yet.
