Running several AI helpers at once: the problem nobody warns you about

AI helpers rarely stall because they aren't smart enough. They stall waiting on you. What the research says about interruptions, and how one screen fixed it.

By the Lighter Hours team·October 23, 2026·2 min read

At first, AI feels like a superpower. Then you have three AI helpers running at once, each one politely waiting for your answer in a different window, and you realize you've built yourself a new job: keeping track of them.

That's the problem nobody warns you about.

Your attention is the bottleneck

  • Microsoft's Work Trend Index found the most-pinged workers are interrupted by a meeting, email, or message about every two minutes.
  • Research by Gloria Mark at UC Irvine, discussed by the American Psychological Association, found people now focus on one screen for about 47 seconds on average before switching, and that faster switching means more stress.
  • The APA's research summary on multitasking notes that switching between tasks can cost as much as 40% of someone's productive time.

Add several AI helpers to that and you've added more things asking for your attention, not fewer. No wonder Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027.

Where the time actually goes

A good AI helper stops and asks before it does anything important. That's the point. But every question is a small wait, and with several helpers spread across windows, a question can sit for an hour while nobody notices.

When we looked at where our own time went, the AI was rarely the slow part. The slow part was a helper sitting idle, waiting for an answer we hadn't seen.

One screen for everything

So we built a single screen that shows every helper, what it's working on, and whether it's waiting on someone, in plain words. We can answer it right there, without hunting for the right window. In its first two days, 36 replies went to waiting helpers straight from that screen. Each of those used to be a small hunt.

The rule didn't change: helpers still stop and wait for a person at the moments that matter, and nothing counts as done until someone says so. The screen doesn't remove the human check. It makes the human check fast.

What this means for a small team

You probably don't need a custom screen on day one. But plan for the moment you have more than one AI helper:

  • Know where each helper asks its questions, and check that place at set times.
  • Give each helper a plain name that says what it does.
  • Decide who answers when a helper is waiting, so it isn't everyone and therefore no one.

AI saves the most time when it never sits waiting on an answer nobody saw.
