You didn't start a business to be the middleman on email

Most of your inbox is waiting on someone else's answer. Here's how we built AI that chases those answers for us, and what the research says it costs.

By the Lighter Hours team·October 30, 2026·3 min read

It's 9 PM and you're still in your inbox. Not because the work is hard. Because one email needs an answer from your bookkeeper, another needs a number from your manager, and a third needs a yes from a partner who hasn't replied since Tuesday. You're not doing the work. You're the switchboard.

We know that feeling well, because we lived in it. So we built our way out of it, and this is what we learned.

It's not just you

The research says this is how most of us spend our days.

  • A McKinsey Global Institute study found office workers spend about 28% of the workweek managing email.
  • Asana's Anatomy of Work research found the average knowledge worker spends about 60% of their time on "work about work": chasing updates, searching for information, and following up.
  • Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index found the most-pinged workers are interrupted by a meeting, email, or message about every two minutes.

Put those together and the picture is clear. The expensive part of a business email is rarely the reply. It's everything around it: figuring out who knows the answer, asking them, remembering to check back, and then piecing the answer together.

What we built instead

We built an AI that does the middleman part for us. Here's what happens when an email comes in that needs someone else:

  • It works out who has the answer. It reads the email and decides which person holds the missing piece.
  • It asks them directly. A short, specific message with the context they need.
  • It watches for their answer, so we don't have to. The moment that person replies, the work picks back up on its own, even if they answer late.
  • It checks before it writes. It pulls the facts from every source it can reach, checks them against the systems they came from, and only then drafts the reply. If something can't be verified, it asks instead of guessing.

The answer itself restarts the work. Nobody has to remember to check.

What changed

We stopped being the switchboard. Questions that used to sit in our inbox for days now move as fast as the person with the answer. And because every reply is built from checked sources, we trust what goes out.

That's the real point of automation for a small business. It's not about replacing anyone. It's about getting your evenings back, and letting your people spend their time on customers instead of chasing each other.

Where to start in your business

You don't need our whole setup to feel the difference. Pick the one email loop that drains you most:

  • Customer questions that need a number from someone else.
  • Vendor or supplier follow-ups that stall.
  • Approvals that wait on one busy person.

Write down who usually has the answer, and where the facts live. That's the map an AI needs. The rest is wiring, with a person approving anything that matters.
